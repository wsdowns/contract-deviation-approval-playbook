# Deal profile

An approver should be able to decide from the page, without picking up the phone. The deal profile gives every approver the same grounding facts about the deal. It is assembled once, when the seller hands over the contract, from the organization's systems and a few answers from the seller. The playbook attaches it to every escalation request.

## What the profile contains, and where it comes from

In practice the profile is assembled, not typed. The playbook pulls what it can from the organization's systems, and the seller supplies only what no system holds. In the example cases it is a file, standing in for those systems.

| Field | What to write | Usual source |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Name, industry and size | CRM account record |
| Deal | New, renewal or expansion; annual fees; term; total contract value | CRM opportunity and the quote |
| Target signature | Date, and why that date | CRM close date; the reason from the seller |
| Strategic or standard | **Strategic** or **Standard**, with one line on why | Account tier set by sales leadership or sales operations, not the seller |
| Upside | Other business this deal could lead to (opportunity, rough value, timing), or "none known" | Other open opportunities in the CRM, plus the seller's forecast |
| Competition | Other vendors in play and where the organization stands | CRM competitor field and deal notes |
| Customer data | The kinds of data involved and whether any is regulated | Solution scope or the security team |
| If we say no | What the seller expects to happen | **The seller** |
| Precedent | Similar concessions granted before, or "none known" | The record custodian's approval records |

The seller also gives **one line per requested change** on why the customer says it needs it.

**What only the seller supplies:** the reason for each requested change, the expectation if the answer is no, and any upside not yet in the CRM.

Precedent closes a loop: the approval records the playbook produces become the precedent log the next profile draws on.

## What the playbook adds to each request

- The change, the standard position and the matrix row.
- **Exposure against the deal.** For example, "a $5M cap on $1M of annual fees is 5 times fees."
- The concessions already approved in this deal, so approvers see the cumulative picture.
- Who else must approve, and the status of the other approvals.
- A recommendation: hold the standard, or offer the smallest concession that could close the point.

## Rules

1. **The seller supplies facts, not a pitch.** Opinions belong in the recommendation, labeled as such.
2. **Unknown means unknown.** A field left blank appears in the request as "not provided". The playbook never fills a gap with a guess, and flags a missing field that matters to the request, such as annual fees for a liability cap.
3. **The approver decides.** The profile informs the decision; it does not make it, and it is not an argument for approving.
4. **One profile per deal.** Every approver sees the same facts, so approvals can be compared and audited.
